2Core findings

2.1  The Order Block structural-gate result (headline finding)

ConfigurationTradesWin rateTotal net return
Baseline — OB-gated (MACD + KDJ + SMC Order Block gate)2770.37%+30.31%
Ablation A — indicators-only, entry-ATR stop14060.00%−14.63%
Ablation B — indicators-only, swing-pivot stop13855.07%−25.50%

Removing the SMC Order Block structural gate and trading the same MACD/KDJ momentum signals directly turns a positive, statistically departing result (one-sided exact binomial p = 0.026 for the baseline) into a losing strategy under both tested stop-placement conventions. A between-arms bootstrap (arm 3 vs. baseline, resampled at n=27) gives an empirical p = 0.0020 for the baseline's avg-return advantage — the strongest single piece of evidence that the OB structural gate itself, not risk-management mechanics (stop placement), drives the strategy's outperformance. This is disclosed as the paper's central methodological contribution, not just a side result (see paper_FINAL_for_docx_handoff.md's abstract).

2.2  Return concentration — 3 of 27 trades = 51.7% of total net return

The three most-concentrated winning trades (entry_idx 359, 6366, 8280) together account for 51.7% of the strategy's total net return, out of only 27 trades total. They are not clustered inside a single market regime under either regime-classification method used (BEAR/BULL/BULL under the calendar method; CHOP/BULL/BULL under the drawdown method) — a modest robustness point — but the concentration itself means the headline return figure is materially sensitive to a handful of outcomes, a caveat the paper states explicitly rather than lets the 70.37% win rate obscure.

2.3  Other headline results, stated precisely

  • Binomial significance: baseline (q≥0) one-sided p = 0.026 (two-sided 0.052); q≥1 flips from 0.038 (n=26, pre-fix) to 0.054 (n=25, post-fix) — a significance-conclusion flip caused by the no-lookahead correction (Known bugs #3, §4).
  • Regime dependence: 85.71% win rate in the strongest regime under both classification methods; 83.3% of total return share comes from the 2024–25 bull phase under the calendar method — the edge is concentrated in bull-trending conditions, not uniform across the window.
  • Fee fragility: the headline win-rate significance claim flips to non-significant (p = 0.124) under a conservative 0.20% round-trip fee-and-slippage assumption, even though the underlying return-magnitude comparisons are mathematically unaffected by that same cost.
  • Benchmark vs. passive (never state without the caveat): over 2022–2026, both weekly DCA (+123.13% → $22,312.94) and lump-sum buy-and-hold (+90.07% → $19,007.32) beat the strategy (+30.31% → $13,417.77) in raw terminal value. The strategy's advantage is risk-adjusted shape (Sharpe 1.114 vs. 0.556/0.564) achieved while holding a position only 2.63% of the time vs. 100% continuous exposure. No surface may state or imply the strategy “beats DCA/BTC” without this exposure-time caveat attached.
  • OB quality criteria: none of the 5 orthogonal criteria (Displacement, LargeBar, FVG, LiqSweep, VolExpansion) cross p=0.05 under Fisher exact or Welch t; every observed subgroup gap is 3–10× smaller than its minimum-detectable-effect (MDE) at 80% power — correctly read as “underpowered to detect an effect this size,” not “no effect exists.”

3Methodology summary

3.1  Backtest design

Data. Binance BTCUSDT 4-hour klines, 2022-01-01 08:00:00 through 2026-01-01 08:00:00 (UTC+8, per Binance's raw millisecond timestamp shifted +8h), 8,767 bars (1,461 days × 6 bars/day + 1 boundary-inclusive endpoint bar — not 8,760, a corrected arithmetic slip). No parameter was fit, swept, or optimized against the outcome being measured; all 8 strategy parameters are pre-registered and fixed.

Strategy — Dynamic-KDJ, MACD, and SMC Order Block gate. Entry gating reads a static full-history KDJ(9,3,3) plus MACD momentum conditions, both required to fire inside an SMC Order Block structural zone (761 detected OBs across the window). The post-entry exit uses a separate, deliberately distinct “Dynamic-KDJ” state machine (kdj_reset_init/update/exit) seeded from the static KDJ value at the entry bar and recursed forward with a period w = entry_idx − triggering_OB_bar, frozen for that trade's life (observed range across the 27 baseline trades: 14–439, median 74). w has zero effect on entry — this two-system split is a disclosed, deliberate design choice, not a bug, and the two KDJ systems must not be unified.

vs. DCA baseline. run_benchmark_vs_passive() compares the OB-gated strategy against weekly dollar-cost-averaging into BTC and a lump-sum buy-and-hold, gross and fee-adjusted, over both the 2022–2026 primary window and the 2018–2022 formulation-period window (explicitly not out-of-sample — it's the window the rules were originally shaped against).

3.2  Ablation study structure

A three-arm design: the OB-gated baseline vs. two indicators-only configurations (MACD + KDJ momentum signals, no SMC Order Block gate) differing only in stop-loss placement — Ablation A uses an entry-anchored ATR stop, Ablation B uses a swing-pivot stop. This isolates whether the OB gate itself (vs. risk-management mechanics) drives the outperformance (§2.1).

Binary-flag scoring replaced the old ordinal composite (Known bug #1, §4). Order Block quality was originally a single 0–5 composite score used as an ordinal “higher is better” threshold; because higher-scored OB sets were nested subsets of lower-scored ones (non-orthogonal), this was replaced with 5 independent per-criterion binary flags (Displacement, LargeBar, FVG, LiqSweep, VolExpansion), each tested separately via Fisher's exact test (true/false vs. win/loss) and Welch's t-test (true vs. false group mean return). The 0–5 score field is still computed and still exists in the data, but is no longer used as an analysis threshold anywhere.

6Defense prep — the 5 custom statistics functions

Every hypothesis test in this codebase (binomial, Fisher exact, Welch t, Pearson, Spearman) is a direct, unmodified scipy.stats call — no defense needed beyond “we called the library function.” These 5 are different: each contains genuine hand-written arithmetic or a hand-rolled resampling loop that a panelist can reasonably ask you to justify by name. Full line-by-line verification, worked examples, and edge cases live in docs/MATH_CORRECTNESS_AND_COMPREHENSION.md; provenance (is this copied from somewhere?) lives in docs/PROVENANCE_VERDICT.md. Verdict from both: no incorrect math, no copied code — every function independently implements a standard, textbook formula. Three assumptions are worth naming unprompted (flagged below).

6.1  Flags to know by name, unprompted

F1Sharpe/Sortino implicitly assume risk-free rate (and MAR) = 0

The code uses raw per-bar returns directly — mathematically equivalent to R_f = 0 in Sharpe's SR = E[R_a − R_f]/σ and T (MAR) = 0 in Sortino's downside-deviation target. Defensible for a crypto strategy with no explicit funding-rate or benchmark leg modeled, but it's a real, nameable deviation from the general textbook formula and is not stated in the docstring. If asked “where's the risk-free rate,” the honest answer is “assumed zero, not subtracted, not documented in code — but mathematically implied.”

F2mde_power_analysis()'s detectable flag reads False, not “undefined,” on NaN input

A subgroup with <2 trades makes np.std(ddof=1) divide by zero → NaN → detectable = abs(diff) >= NaN silently evaluates False in Python (any comparison against NaN is False) rather than raising or flagging itself as unusable. Does not occur on real data — the 5 OB-quality criteria actually used have subgroup sizes n=4..23, never <2. Worth naming if asked “what happens with a 1-trade subgroup” rather than letting it sound like an unknown gap.

F3Bootstrap CI is plain percentile, not bias-corrected (BCa)

Uses Efron's original 1981/82 percentile method (raw 2.5th/97.5th percentiles of the resample distribution), not the later bias-corrected-and-accelerated (BCa) refinement that corrects for skew. For a 27-trade sample with a right-skewed return distribution, the plain percentile CI can be mildly liberal/conservative relative to BCa. Standard, disclosed-as-standard simplification (the docstring already says so) — named here so “BCa” is ready as the specific term if a panelist asks “why not a corrected bootstrap.”

AI-assistance disclosure — say this unprompted

Per PROVENANCE_VERDICT.md: none of these 5 functions (nor the 5 direct scipy.stats wrappers) were copied from a specific paper's or repository's implementation — each independently implements its formula from public documentation and standard references. But every commit that introduced or materially edited them carries a Co-Authored-By: Claude Sonnet 5 trailer. The accurate answer to “did you personally write this” is: directed the design (which test, which populations, which parameters), drafted with AI assistance rather than typed unaided, then verified against known-good regression fixtures — distinct from both “wrote it alone” and “copied a paper's code,” and should be stated in those terms rather than left for the panel to assume either extreme.
